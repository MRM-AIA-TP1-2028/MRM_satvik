How tracker.py works

The program reads a webcam, finds blue objects and a tennis ball in every frame, and measures them in centimetres using an ArUco marker of known size. The map below is the whole program: startup runs once, then the main loop repeats for every frame.

Teaching it

Explain it in this order

  1. The goal. Each frame, find blue things of any shape and one tennis ball, keep the two separate, and give their sizes in cm.
  2. Colour, not shape, finds things. The frame is blurred to cut noise, then converted to HSV, where hue is the colour itself. A shadow changes brightness (V) but barely changes hue, so one hue range catches the object under different lighting. Blue is hue 100–130, the ball's yellow-green is 22–43.
  3. Mask, clean, outline. inRange turns the frame into a black-and-white mask. Morphology tidies it: opening removes specks, closing fills holes. findContours then gives the outline of each white blob.
  4. Blue vs ball. Every big-enough blue outline is accepted, because blue objects can be any shape. A ball candidate must also be round: it is rejected unless its circularity and fill ratio are high enough.
  5. Pixels to centimetres. The ArUco marker has a known real size. Its side in pixels divided by its side in cm gives px per cm. Lengths are divided by that number, and areas by its square.
  6. Tracking. Each frame's blue objects are matched to last frame's by nearest centre. That gives each object a stable ID, a trail and a running total of the distance it has travelled.
  7. Display. The Camera window draws on the real image. The Info window repeats the numbers on a black background. Clicking a blue object retunes the blue colour range to that exact shade.

The maths, in one place

Scale

px_per_cm = mean marker side (px) ÷ marker_cm

Then smoothed each frame: new = 0.7 × old + 0.3 × measured.

Pixels to centimetres

cm = px ÷ px_per_cm
cm² = px² ÷ px_per_cm²

Area divides by the square, because both width and height shrink.

Centre of a shape

x = m10 ÷ m00, y = m01 ÷ m00

From cv2.moments. m00 is the area.

Is it round?

circularity = 4πA ÷ P²
fill = A ÷ (πr²)

A circle scores 1 on both. Ball needs ≥ 0.6 and ≥ 0.5.

Position relative to the ball

dx = x_blue − x_ball
dy = y_blue − y_ball
d = √(dx² + dy²)

+x is right and +y is down, as in image coordinates.

Distance travelled

total += step, if step ≥ 3 px

Small steps are camera jitter and are ignored, so a still object stays at 0.
